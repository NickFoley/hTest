The wall

Why it's arranged this way

The whole design is one decision repeated: sort storage by height, not by type.

0 → 910Self-serve, age 2+

Open bins and front-facing books. Nothing here has a door, a lid or a catch. A toddler can pull a bin out, tip it on the rug and shove it back one-handed. This is the only band that decides whether the room stays tidy, so it gets the full 4150 of width and the deepest carcass.

910 → 1750The band you actually see

Oak bench, open shelves, the screen. Eye level for a standing adult and reaching height for a 5–8 year old. Books with their covers out, a few good objects, the TV in its recess. Keep it deliberately under-filled — this band is the room's face.

1750 → 2600Adults only

Seven doors, nothing on show. Board games, craft supplies, spare Duplo, the toys on rotation, the things with 200 pieces. 1.2 m³ of it. Above kid sightline, so visual noise disappears without anything having to be thrown out.

01 — SYMMETRY

Two matching towers and a centred screen. The eye reads the outline before the contents, and a symmetric outline reads as joinery rather than furniture pushed against a wall.

02 — CONTINUOUS LINES

Five horizontals run the full width: 560, 950, 1230, 1500, 1750. Unbroken lines are the single biggest difference between "built in" and "bought".

03 — NEUTRAL BINS

Every bin the same quiet greige. Rainbow bins are what make a playroom look chaotic. Let the colour come from the books and whatever's being played with.

04 — DARK BEHIND THE TV

A deep green slatted panel swallows the screen instead of framing it. When it's off it reads as a dark recess, not a black rectangle.

Finish notes

  • Doors and carcasses a half-tone warmer than the wall, matt. Painting them the exact wall colour flattens the wall; a half-tone off gives the joinery an edge without making it loud.
  • Oak on every horizontal you touch — bench, shelf fronts, ledge lips, the finger pull on each door. It ties to the floor and takes knocks.
  • The green panel is the only dark thing in the room. 18 mm painted MDF with 45 mm battens at 46 mm centres, or a ready-made acoustic slat panel.

Hardware

  • Push-to-open on all seven upper doors. No handles at head height, nothing to catch a forehead.
  • Soft-close hinges throughout.
  • LED strip at 1750 under the bridge, warm white 2700 K, washing down over the TV panel and the bench. Wire it to a wall switch, not a remote you'll lose.

Build notes

Before anything is ordered

  • Measure the wall at floor, 1000 and 2400 — walls in this kind of house are rarely square, and the 2 × 50 scribes are sized to swallow about 40 mm of error. More than that and the bays need resizing.
  • Check the cornice projection. The joinery stops at 2600 with a shadow gap to the cornice; if the cornice drops lower than 2630 the door band shortens, not the bins.
  • Remove the skirting behind the full run so carcasses sit tight to the wall.
  • Confirm TROFAST frame and bin sizes against current stock before you plan around them — the range changes, and the drawing assumes 42 × 30 bins in a 450 deep carcass.

Services and safety

  • Recessed outlet behind the TV at ~1100, plus a conduit down to a second outlet behind the centre low unit for the console and soundbar. Do this before anything is fixed.
  • Anchor every carcass into studs — this is a wall children will climb whether or not they're allowed to. Nothing freestanding, no exceptions.
  • TV mount into studs, not plasterboard anchors, and a slim fixed plate so the screen sits close to the panel.
  • Leave ~80 mm between the low unit top at 560 and the bottom of the screen at 680 for soundbar and airflow.

Making it stay tidy

The storage is only half of it. These four habits are what the layout is actually built around.

  • Photo labels, not words. A printed photo of the bin's contents on the label card, so a pre-reader can put things back without asking. This matters more than any other detail here.
  • One bin, one category, never full. A bin at 70% gets tidied; a bin at 100% gets tipped out and abandoned. If a category outgrows its bin, it goes up into the door band, not into a second bin.
  • Rotate from above. The door band holds the toys that aren't in play. Swap a bin's worth every few weeks — old toys come back interesting, and the floor load stays constant.
  • Books face out at the bottom, spine out at the top. Covers sell books to a four-year-old; spines store them. That's why the front-facing zone is deliberately small and low, and the bulk library sits above it.

What to do with the TROFAST you already own

You have roughly 4 metres of frames on this wall today and the design reuses most of them: every bin column in the drawing is a TROFAST frame behind a painted front. The surplus — the narrow 460 frames and the odd low units — is worth keeping intact for bedrooms or the garage rather than being forced into the 1100 modules, where it will produce awkward fillers. Decide that before the joiner quotes, because it changes whether the lower band is reused boxes or new carcasses.
